# Drawing a grammar of marks

When a style is made of marks (engraved lines, hatching, stipple, woodcut, brush strokes), the
marks are generated by a rule and never traced: tracing them yields fragments by the hundred
thousand and keeps every accident the grammar should drop. The wavy-line engraving
(`portraits/pieces/wavy-line-engraving/`, its `engrave.ts` above all) is the worked example of
laying tone by rule. Its face and hair still come from a traced line field, and its silhouettes
from the plates' alpha: do not copy those.

## Pixels enter as fields

- **Tone:** how much of the paper the ink must cover to give, seen from afar, the light the
  reference gives there. The eye averages light, so match in linear light, not in sRGB values.
  Blur the reference past its own marks first, and weight the blur by opacity so the transparent
  paper round a layer does not wash its edges out.
- **Shape:** silhouettes, authored or read off gridded crops, that the marks are cut along.
- **Direction and depth,** where the grammar uses them: a hatch that follows a surface, spacing
  that changes with distance.
- **What is hidden.** A layer's tone field covers what nearer layers hide and reaches past the
  frame by the bleed (`motion.md`). Fill the hidden tone (`trace/fill.ts`) before laying marks, or a
  parted plane shows bare paper where it was covered.

Nothing else comes from pixels. A mark that copies a line in the reference is a trace.

## One family of marks

- One rule lays every mark of a kind across the frame, so they read as one hand: lines where a
  function crosses whole numbers of spacings, streamlines of a direction field, dots spaced by a
  rule. Seed any randomness, so every build lays the same marks.
- The one family built is the engraving's level lines. A family that follows a direction is new
  work, built in the piece. The engraving's cuts and tapers are sized for its spacing and stepped
  along the frame's x, which suits near-level lines only: re-derive them for a family that turns.
- The grammar says what the family may do. If the style keeps its lines level, they stay level
  everywhere and form is carried by width and edges; lines turned with the form are another style.

## Tone by the mark's own variable

- Each grammar carries tone in one or two variables: a line's width, the spacing, the length of
  its breaks, layers of crossing, a dot's size. Solve them so the coverage over a patch equals the
  tone field, after the grammar's own compression of it (`grammar.md`).
- Rendering adds a gain: a mark comes out wider than it is set, as a printed dot does. Measure it
  on a dark stretch of a render and take it off every mark, or every tone shifts darker.
- The extremes show the craft. Where it is dark, the paper between marks narrows into tapered
  slivers rather than closing up; where it is light, a mark breaks into tapered dashes rather than
  thinning to a hair. Every end tapers to a point; nothing is cut square.

## Edges and form

- A silhouette cuts the family cleanly along a smooth edge; marks never fray into a neighbor.
- Form lines (contours, seams, features, hair) are strokes of their own over the family: a curve
  through a few points read off the reference, tapering at both ends.

## Writing it out

- A few long marks are written as strokes or outlines. Many marks, or marks whose width changes
  along them, are sampled as an ink field at a few samples per pixel and traced to paths
  (`trace/contour.ts`), so a line a pixel wide keeps its shape and a sloping stretch is no thicker
  than a level one.
- Each layer lays its own opaque paper under its marks, cut to its silhouette, so a parted plane
  reads as a print, not as loose marks over the layers behind.
- Keep each layer able to build alone in its own frame, and each stage switchable: the lay-in as
  flat tone steps, then tone in marks, then the form lines.

## Moving groups

Lay every group's marks from the one family, in the frame's coordinates, and cut each group by
its own silhouette: at rest the marks run on across the cut as if it were not there. A symbol
scaled or turned carries its marks with it and leaves the family; keep symbols for things small
enough that their marks read as texture.

## Judging

Compare tone with the marks blurred away (`verify.md`); a line shifted half a period is not an
error. Then judge the craft at 2:1: every end tapered, every join closed, no stray fragment.
